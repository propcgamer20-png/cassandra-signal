Your place vs the planet

Here, annual temperature has changed by +0.39 °C/decade since 1981 (NASA POWER). Over the same years the whole planet warmed by +0.21 °C/decade and the 0-24°S latitude band by +0.14 °C/decade (NASA GISTEMP v4, annual means, 1981–2025). In 2025 the planet was +1.19 °C warmer than its 1951–1980 average.

GISTEMP blends land and ocean and covers whole years; POWER covers one point and one season, so the numbers are context, not a like-for-like comparison.

Would it have warned? Backtests on real events

We rerun the whole trial using only the data that existed before each event: the record is cut at the end of the year before (or before the season starts, whichever is earlier), and the baseline, the fitted noise and the 1,000 histories are all rebuilt from that shorter record. Nothing after the cut-off is used.

São Paulo water crisis, 2014 (Annual rain, 2014) · source

Would the signal have warned? Yes. Before the event the record already showed a drier change that natural variation could not explain (probably a real change).

Using data up to 2013-12 (33 seasons): Probably a real change · trend −7.5% of normal/decade · outside 959 of 1,000 histories.

What happened: 53% of normal rain (863 mm, SPI -3.3), the driest of the 34 years since 1981; drier than 99.8% of the years in 1,000 natural histories built only from the data before it. It stands out as extreme in this dataset.

Our seasonal forecast method, run with the same data, would have said:

Climatology says 33% each. It gave 66% to below normal.
