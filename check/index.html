Check any place yourself

Pick any point on Earth and a season. Your browser downloads that point's monthly NASA POWER record (1981 to now) and puts it on trial with the same method as our cards: it builds 1,000 imaginary histories of natural ups and downs, ranks the real trend among them and gives a verdict. Nothing here is pre-computed, and nothing is sent to us.

      What happens when you press the button

      1. Your browser asks the NASA POWER API for monthly temperature and rain at your point.
      2. Each month is compared with its 1981–2010 average and averaged (or, for rain, totalled) over your season.
      3. The trend is measured with Sen's slope, the median slope between every pair of years.
      4. The trend line is removed and what is left, the natural ups and downs, is modelled. 1,000 new records are made from that model. None of them contains a trend.
      5. If the real trend is bigger than in 950, 990 or all 1,000 of them, the verdict says so. Otherwise it says "not yet". A second, different kind of imaginary history (IAAFT) is used as a cross-check.
      6. Optionally, the checker tests itself on your place: fake records with no trend should be flagged only about 5% of the time.
